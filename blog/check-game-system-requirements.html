Can My PC Run It? How to Check Game System Requirements

February 16, 2026 • 12 min read

🎮 Check Your PC Specs Now

First, find out what hardware you have — then compare it to the game's requirements.

Check GPU → Full Specs →

Nothing is more frustrating than buying a game only to discover your PC can't run it. Whether you're eyeing the latest AAA release or an indie gem, knowing how to check system requirements — and understand what they actually mean — is an essential skill for any PC gamer.

This guide will teach you how to find your PC's specs, where to find game requirements, how to compare them, and what to do if you're on the edge of compatibility.

Step 1: Find Your PC's Specifications

Before comparing to game requirements, you need to know what's in your PC. Here are the key specs that matter:

🎮 GPU (Graphics Card)

The most important component for gaming. Use our GPU Detector to find yours.

🧠 CPU (Processor)

Handles game logic, AI, and physics. Check in Settings → System → About (Windows).

💾 RAM (Memory)

Affects how much the game can keep loaded. Most games need 8-16 GB.

💿 Storage

Games need 50-150 GB now. SSDs provide faster load times than HDDs.

Quick ways to check your specs:

  • Use our tools: Full Specs page shows GPU, RAM, CPU cores, and more
  • Windows: Settings → System → About (CPU, RAM) and Device Manager → Display adapters (GPU)
  • Task Manager: Ctrl+Shift+Esc → Performance tab (CPU, GPU, RAM details)
  • DirectX Diagnostic: Press Win+R, type dxdiag, check Display tab

For detailed guidance on finding your GPU, see our complete guide: How to Check What Graphics Card You Have.

Step 2: Find the Game's System Requirements

Game requirements are usually listed on:

  • Steam: Scroll down on the game's store page to "System Requirements"
  • Epic Games Store: Listed at the bottom of the game page
  • GOG: Found in the "System requirements" section
  • Publisher's website: Usually on the game's official page
  • PC Gaming Wiki: Community-maintained, often has more accurate info

Pro tip: Always check the store where you're buying. Requirements can differ slightly between platforms, and some stores list more detailed specs than others.

Understanding Minimum vs. Recommended Requirements

Games typically list two sets of specifications:

⚠️ Minimum Requirements

  • • Game will launch and run
  • • Expect low graphics settings
  • • 720p-1080p resolution
  • • 30 FPS target (often lower)
  • • Playable, but not pretty

✅ Recommended Requirements

  • • Comfortable gaming experience
  • • Medium to high settings
  • • 1080p resolution
  • • 60 FPS target
  • • How the devs intended it to look

Important: "Minimum" doesn't mean "good experience" — it means "technically runs." If you're at minimum specs, expect to lower settings significantly and tolerate occasional stuttering.

Step 3: Compare Your Specs to Requirements

Now for the actual comparison. Here's how to evaluate each component:

Comparing GPUs

GPU comparison is the trickiest because model numbers don't directly indicate performance. A newer mid-range card often beats an older high-end card.

How to compare:

  • Use benchmark databases: Sites like PassMark, TechPowerUp, or Tom's Hardware GPU Hierarchy
  • Search "[your GPU] vs [required GPU]": Often shows direct comparisons
  • Look at the generation: GTX 1660 Super ≈ GTX 1070 despite lower number
  • Check VRAM: Modern games increasingly need 6-8+ GB

GPU Naming Quick Guide

NVIDIA: Higher first digit = newer generation. Higher second digits = more powerful within generation.

RTX 4070 > RTX 3070 > RTX 2070 (same tier, newer gen is better)

RTX 4080 > RTX 4070 > RTX 4060 (same gen, higher tier is better)

AMD: Similar pattern — RX 7800 XT > RX 6800 XT > RX 5700 XT

Comparing CPUs

CPUs are somewhat easier — more cores and higher clock speeds generally mean better performance. But architecture matters too.

  • Core count: Most games use 4-6 cores effectively; more helps with streaming/multitasking
  • Clock speed: Higher GHz is better for gaming (3.5+ GHz is good)
  • Generation: Intel 12th/13th/14th gen or AMD Ryzen 5000/7000 series are current
  • Use benchmarks: PassMark CPU or Tom's Hardware CPU Hierarchy

Example CPU Comparison

Game requires: Intel Core i5-8400 or AMD Ryzen 5 2600

You have: Intel Core i5-12400

✅ You're fine — 12th gen i5 significantly outperforms 8th gen i5

Comparing RAM

RAM is straightforward — you need at least as much as required:

  • 8 GB: Minimum for modern gaming; many games now require this
  • 16 GB: The sweet spot for 2024-2026; recommended for most games
  • 32 GB: Future-proof, helpful for streaming and content creation

Note: RAM speed (e.g., DDR4-3200 vs DDR4-2400) matters less for gaming than total amount, but faster RAM does provide a small benefit, especially on AMD systems.

Storage Requirements

Modern games are huge. Check that you have enough space:

  • AAA games: Often 80-150 GB or more
  • SSD vs HDD: SSDs dramatically reduce load times; some games now require SSDs
  • DirectStorage: Newest games may require NVMe SSD for optimal performance

What If You're Below Requirements?

Don't give up! Here are options if your PC falls short:

Optimization Tricks

  • Lower resolution: Dropping from 1080p to 900p or 720p helps significantly
  • Reduce demanding settings: Shadows, anti-aliasing, ray tracing, draw distance
  • Use FSR/DLSS: AMD FSR or NVIDIA DLSS can boost FPS with minimal quality loss
  • Close background apps: Free up RAM and CPU resources
  • Update drivers: GPU drivers often include game-specific optimizations
  • Check PCGamingWiki: Community tweaks for better performance

Settings That Impact Performance Most

Setting Performance Impact Visual Impact
Resolution Very High High
Ray Tracing Very High Medium-High
Shadow Quality High Medium
Anti-Aliasing Medium-High Medium
Draw Distance Medium Low-Medium
Texture Quality Low (VRAM dependent) High

Best strategy: Start with a preset (Low or Medium), then selectively increase settings you care about while monitoring FPS.

Cloud Gaming Alternative

If your PC can't run a game, cloud gaming services let you stream games from powerful remote servers:

  • NVIDIA GeForce NOW: Play games you already own on Steam/Epic
  • Xbox Cloud Gaming: Included with Game Pass Ultimate
  • Amazon Luna: Subscription-based streaming
  • Shadow PC: Full Windows PC in the cloud

Special Cases and Considerations

Laptop Gaming

Laptop GPUs are generally slower than desktop versions with the same name:

  • A laptop RTX 4060 performs worse than a desktop RTX 4060
  • Thermal throttling can reduce performance during long sessions
  • Check if your laptop has a dedicated GPU or just integrated graphics
  • Make sure you're plugged in — laptops throttle on battery

Integrated Graphics

Intel UHD/Iris or AMD Radeon integrated graphics can play older and less demanding games, but struggle with modern titles. If requirements list a dedicated GPU (like GTX 1050), integrated graphics usually won't cut it — though some games run surprisingly well on AMD's APUs (like Ryzen 7 7840U).

VR Requirements

VR games have higher requirements because they render two views at high frame rates:

  • Most VR requires GTX 1060 / RX 580 minimum
  • Recommended: RTX 2070 / RX 5700 XT or better
  • 90 FPS minimum (vs 30-60 for flat games)
  • Check headset-specific requirements too

Frequently Asked Questions

My specs are between minimum and recommended. Will I be okay?

Yes! You'll likely be able to play at medium settings around 45-60 FPS at 1080p. Experiment with settings to find your preferred balance of visuals and performance.

The game lists a GPU I've never heard of. How do I compare?

Search for benchmark comparisons. Older or region-specific GPUs might have different names. Tom's Hardware GPU hierarchy and UserBenchmark can help translate unfamiliar model names.

Can I upgrade just my GPU to meet requirements?

Usually yes, but check for bottlenecks. A very old CPU might limit a new GPU. Also verify your power supply can handle the new card. For laptops, GPU upgrades aren't possible.

Why do some games run fine below minimum specs?

Publishers often list conservative requirements. Many games have excellent optimization or scalable settings that let them run on weaker hardware than officially stated.

Should I trust "Can You Run It" websites?

They're useful starting points but not always accurate. They may not account for driver optimizations, game patches, or the nuances of similar hardware. Manual comparison is more reliable.

Summary: Your Pre-Purchase Checklist

  1. Find your specs: Use our Full Specs tool or check Windows settings
  2. Find game requirements: Check Steam, Epic, or the official website
  3. Compare GPU: Use benchmark sites — this is the most important component
  4. Compare CPU: Make sure you meet minimum core count and performance
  5. Check RAM: 16 GB is safe for almost everything in 2026
  6. Verify storage: Have enough space, preferably on an SSD
  7. Read reviews: Search "[game name] performance" for real-world reports
  8. Have a backup plan: Know Steam's refund policy (under 2 hours playtime)
